# The Oval Table: Poker University Mastermind Council

Nov 20, 2026 · @Ryan Grissinger

## Why an oval table

Twenty players, five eras, one curriculum. A round table says everyone is equal; an oval admits there are two ends, one for instinct and feel, one for math and theory, with the best seats in the middle where they meet. That tension is the university's whole philosophy: read people and respect the numbers.

Each seat below names the player and the ideas they would bring to the table, based on how they played and what they published.

## The founding four (head of the table)

- **Doyle Brunson.** Aggression wins, and the player who puts the pressure on controls the hand. Super/System (1979) was the first time a top pro opened his playbook, so Doyle brings the idea that the university should teach openly, not hoard secrets. Also: bankroll survival over a 60-year career.
- **Daniel Negreanu.** Hand reading and "small ball," playing many pots cheaply and outplaying people after the flop. After a slump in the late 2010s he rebuilt his game around solver study, so he argues for blending feel with theory instead of picking a side. Brings transparency too, since he shares his results and reasoning publicly.
- **Phil Hellmuth.** Patience and survival in tournaments, folding hands others can't, plus reading opponents (his "white magic"). Holds the record for WSOP bracelets. His seat also covers brand and showmanship, and his famous blowups make him the honest case study for the tilt and emotional control course.
- **Phil Ivey.** Total focus at the table and mastery of every game, not just Hold'em. Ivey brings the standard: mixed games, the highest stakes, and an unreadable presence. His seat argues the university should produce complete players, not Hold'em specialists.

## The old guard (instinct end)

- **Stu Ungar.** Raw talent: three Main Event titles (1980, 1981, 1997) and near-perfect reads. His life is also the cautionary tale, so his seat sponsors the course on addiction, money management, and protecting the player behind the cards.
- **Johnny Chan.** Closing. Back-to-back Main Events in 1987 and 1988 make him the voice of heads-up play and finishing a tournament once you get there.
- **Chip Reese.** Doyle called him the best all-around player he ever saw, and he won the first $50,000 H.O.R.S.E. title in 2006. Brings cash game discipline, game selection, and the calm temperament that keeps a winner winning.
- **Amarillo Slim.** Storytelling and showmanship. Slim sold poker to the public on talk shows after his 1972 Main Event win, so he speaks for making the game, and the university, entertaining and accessible.
- **Johnny Moss.** The road gambler and first World Series champion (1970). Brings history: where the game came from, and the street smarts of a time when collecting your winnings was part of the skill.

## The thinkers (theory end)

- **David Sklansky.** The Fundamental Theorem of Poker: every time you play a hand differently than you would if you could see your opponent's cards, they gain. The Theory of Poker is the obvious first-year textbook, and Sklansky's seat insists every decision is measured in expected value, not results.
- **Dan Harrington.** Tournament structure as a science. He won the 1995 Main Event, made back-to-back final tables in 2003 and 2004, and taught a generation with Harrington on Hold'em. Brings the "M" ratio and the idea that your stack size, not your cards, sets your strategy.

## The modern greats (middle of the table)

- **Erik Seidel.** Longevity. He lost the 1988 Main Event to Chan (the hand in Rounders) and kept winning at the top for 35 more years. Brings humility, quiet study, and the habit of adapting every time the game changes.
- **Justin Bonomo.** Self-directed study. His 2018 run, including the $1 million Big One for One Drop, pushed him to the top of the all-time earnings list. His seat argues for structured study plans and honest review of your own hands.
- **Bryn Kenney.** High-roller economics: staking, buying action, and managing variance at the biggest buy-ins. He once led the all-time money list after a single $20 million score in 2019. Also brings mindset and confidence work.
- **Stephen Chidwick.** Preparation and composure. Respected by peers as one of the most complete tournament players alive, with a calm, math-first style. His seat stands for doing the homework before the game, not during it.
- **Fedor Holz.** Mental performance. After a historic 2016 he stepped back, talked openly about burnout, and co-founded a mental training app. Brings the curriculum on focus, routines, and knowing when to step away.

## The solver era (theory end)

- **Jason Koon.** Rigorous solver study paired with athletic training. His seat argues the body is part of the edge: sleep, fitness, and stamina for long sessions belong in the curriculum.
- **Linus Loeliger.** Pure theory at the highest online cash stakes, where he spent years ranked at or near the top. Brings game theory optimal play as the baseline every student should know before they start deviating from it.
- **Phil Galfond.** Teaching itself. He built the Run It Once training site and turns complex ideas into lessons people can use. His seat runs pedagogy: how to sequence lessons, coach, and build a learning community.
- **Vanessa Selbst.** Fearless aggression and cross-discipline thinking. The most accomplished woman in tournament history (three bracelets) holds a Yale law degree and later moved to a hedge fund. Brings the case that poker skills transfer to business and life, and that the table should welcome everyone.

## From seats to schools

The council naturally splits into six schools. Each needs at least one voice from each end of the oval.

| School | Lead seats | Core idea |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundations and Theory | Sklansky, Loeliger, Galfond | Expected value and GTO as the baseline |
| Reading People | Negreanu, Hellmuth, Ungar | Hand reading, tells, and exploiting deviations |
| Tournament Strategy | Harrington, Chan, Chidwick, Seidel | Stack depth, structure, and closing |
| Cash and Mixed Games | Ivey, Reese, Brunson | Game selection and complete-player skills |
| Mind and Body | Holz, Koon, Ungar | Tilt control, fitness, and recovery |
| Business of Poker | Kenney, Bonomo, Amarillo Slim, Selbst | Bankroll, staking, brand, and transferable skills |

Open question for the founders: does the university teach live play, online play, or both, and which end of the oval leads year one?
