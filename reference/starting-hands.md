# Starting Hands: Sklansky-Malmuth Groups

A Texas Hold'em starting point, not a rulebook. Sklansky and Malmuth ranked the 169 starting hands into eight groups by strength. Lower group number means a stronger hand. "s" means suited; no "s" means offsuit. "x" means any small card.

## The Groups

| Group | Hands | Rough guide |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AA, KK, QQ, JJ, AKs | Raise from any position |
| 2 | TT, AQs, AJs, KQs, AK | Raise from any position |
| 3 | 99, JTs, QJs, KJs, ATs, AQ | Playable early, raise most spots |
| 4 | T9s, KQ, 88, QTs, 98s, J9s, AJ, KTs | Middle position and later |
| 5 | 77, 87s, Q9s, T8s, KJ, QJ, JT, 76s, 97s, Axs, 65s | Middle to late position |
| 6 | 66, AT, 55, 86s, KT, QT, 54s, K9s, J8s, 75s | Late position |
| 7 | 44, J9, 64s, T9, 53s, 33, 98, 43s, 22, Kxs, T7s, Q8s | Late position, loose or passive games |
| 8 | 87, A9, Q9, 76, 42s, 32s, 96s, 85s, J8, J7s, 65, 54, 74s, K9, T8 | Button or blinds, very favorable spots only |

Anything not listed is a fold in nearly every situation.

## Where Good Players Deviate

The groups were built for full-ring limit hold'em. Strong players treat them as a baseline and adjust for these factors.

**Position.** The biggest lever. Early position gets roughly Groups 1 to 3. Each seat closer to the button opens the range. On the button, good players open far wider than Group 8 when the blinds are weak.

**Table size.** Fewer players means fewer hands to beat you. Six-max ranges are much wider than full ring, and heads-up players raise most hands. A hand like A7o goes from fold to open.

**No-limit implied odds.** The groups favor high-card strength because limit caps what you can win. In no-limit, small pairs and suited connectors climb in value since they can win a whole stack when they hit a set or a disguised straight. Offsuit hands like KJ and QT drop, because they make second-best hands that lose big pots.

**Stack depth.** Deep stacks (100 big blinds and up) reward speculative hands that need to hit. Short stacks shift value back to high cards and pairs that play well all-in preflop. Set mining with 22 needs roughly 15 to 20 times the call behind to be worth it.

**Opponents.** Against tight, passive players, steal more and widen late. Against loose callers, tighten up and value bet strong hands since bluffs fail. Aggressive players behind you mean fewer marginal opens, because you will face more 3-bets.

**Image and balance.** Playing only Group 1 and 2 hands makes you readable. Good players mix in some suited connectors and suited aces as raises (and occasional 3-bet bluffs) so opponents cannot put them on a narrow range. Modern solver-based play builds these mixed ranges deliberately.

## Bottom Line

Learn the groups first. They teach discipline and keep beginners out of trouble. Then adjust for position, table size, game type, stack depth, and opponents. Most leaks come from playing Groups 6 to 8 out of position.
