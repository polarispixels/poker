This book is a path, not a library. The books below are where the council's ideas were first written down, and each one goes deeper than a chapter can. Read them in the order of the book's six parts, and keep the tables here open while you study.

The path at a glance

The book has 38 chapters in six parts. Study in that order. Each part has a short list of books that match it, so you never read a book for a skill you have not used yet.

Books by part

Each book is listed by title and author only, with no year or edition. Editions change, so check the current one before you buy. A book may be newer or older than the ideas in the matching chapter; where they differ, trust the chapter's numbers and then test the book's claim yourself.

Several of the classics were written before solvers, and some for limit hold'em, where bets come in fixed sizes. Their thinking still holds up. Their exact hand charts and bet sizes often do not carry over to a live $2/$5 No-Limit game with 100 big blind stacks, so check them against the matching chapter.

The council's seats and their books

Three council members are tied to a published book in the council notes. For the rest, read the idea in the chapter and look for the player's own teaching.

A study loop that works with any book

Reading is half the job. Play a few sessions, mark the hands that confused you, and read with those hands in mind. A book you read with a question in hand teaches more than one you read cover to cover.

What Justin Bonomo might say

He would tell you to write the plan down: which part you are on, which book goes with it, how many sessions before you move on, and a fixed hour each week to review your own hands with no excuses.

An imagined voice: our reading of the structured study and honest self-review his council seat stands for, not his words.

Key idea

One book at a time, matched to the part you are in. Finish it, play, and review before you start the next.
